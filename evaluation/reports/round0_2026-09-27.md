## Interpretation (Curation & Evaluation Lead #1)
1. **The technical filter is safe.** No wanted photo was excluded for a technical reason: M1 = 0/68 must, M2 = 0/41 special moments (upper 95% bounds: 5% and 9%), and the held-out part agrees. Every technical removal in the sample was labeled "no" (screenshot 17/17, exposure 13/13, low quality 2/2). The ADR-021 conservatism holds on real data.
2. **Duplicates need their own check.** 33 of the 36 sampled duplicate alternates are labeled wanted. That's fine *if* the keeper shows the same moment. But if near-duplicate grouping merges different moments (bursts, pose changes), moments are being hidden. The seed labeled single photos, not groups, so M5 can't be measured yet. Next: a small group review (~15 groups): "is this really the same photo? which copy?".
3. **Junk retention is the main gap, and it's expected.** 36 of 71 "no" photos pass the filter; about 24% of the kept sample is unwanted. These are content judgments (boring, irrelevant), which classical rules can't see. This is the baseline that round 1 (semantic relevance, 7a) must beat without raising M1/M2.
4. **Calibration headroom (low priority):** the technical flags are 100% precise on this sample, so they're probably too lenient rather than too strict (this matches the blur follow-up in ROADMAP). Loosen them only with more "no"-labeled data; the must-never-lose rule comes first.
5. **Data notes:** 1 orphan label (photo no longer in the library). Correction to an earlier PM message: the "10 must+special in the filtered set" are duplicate alternates, not technical exclusions, so they're not lost moments.
6. **Limits:** n = 220 from one library; the `filtered` stratum is over-sampled, so the rates describe the sample, not the whole library. The per-reason table is the reliable view.
